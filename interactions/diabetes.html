Supplements and diabetes medicines

Talk to a pharmacist before you change anything.

This is an educational list of interactions tied to a public source. It is not a complete medication review and it is not medical advice. Ask a pharmacist, or the clinician who prescribes the medicine, before you start, stop, or change a supplement. A pair that is missing here is not a safety clearance. Berberine is sold as a blood-sugar supplement. In people with type 2 diabetes, a meta-analysis found that adding it to glucose-lowering drugs lowered blood sugar more than either one alone. That is a reason to tell the prescriber, not a reason to adjust a dose on your own.

The paper studied people with type 2 diabetes who were taking glucose-lowering drugs. It does not test every newer diabetes medicine, and it does not prove the combination is unsafe for every person.

Ask before you combine

Berberine plus diabetes drugs lowered blood sugar more than either alone

A 2019 systematic review and meta-analysis in Endocrine Journal (PMID 30393248) looked at berberine in people with type 2 diabetes. It found that berberine combined with glucose-lowering drugs reduced blood sugar more than either berberine or the drugs alone. A bigger drop is a reason the prescriber should know. Do not change an insulin or tablet dose to "make room" for berberine on your own. The paper does not test every newer diabetes drug.

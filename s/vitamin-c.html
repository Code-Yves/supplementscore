Vitamin C (liposomal)

📅 Last reviewed: May 23, 2026 · Editorial team
Immunity · Absorption · Antioxidant
Tier 2 — Promising

Vitamin C (megadose) is a tier 2 (promising evidence) supplement with a SupplementScore composite rating of 62/100. Cochrane found high-dose vitamin C does not prevent colds in the general public, though it may shorten them by about 8%. Typical dose: 200–500 mg/day is optimal.

What it is

Liposomal encapsulation wraps vitamin C in fat-based vesicles that bypass normal gut absorption limits. A 2024 trial found liposomal vitamin C achieves significantly higher blood levels than standard vitamin C at the same dose, approaching intravenous levels. Especially useful for people who get diarrhoea from standard vitamin C above 500 mg. More expensive than regular ascorbic acid. Choose products with verified liposomal encapsulation.

Frequently asked about Vitamin C (megadose)

What is Vitamin C (megadose)?

Cochrane found high-dose vitamin C does not prevent colds in the general public, though it may shorten them by about 8%.

How much Vitamin C (megadose) should I take?

200–500 mg/day is optimal; absorption maxes out around 400 mg per dose; doses above 2 g/day increase kidney stone risk

When should I take Vitamin C (megadose) and what should I know?

Take with food or water at any time of day. Spread doses throughout the day for consistent levels. Avoid mega-doses if prone to kidney stones. Pairs well with iron supplements.

What is the evidence rating for Vitamin C (megadose)?

Vitamin C (megadose) has a SupplementScore composite rating of 62/100 (Tier 2 — promising evidence). The rating combines efficacy, safety, research depth, onset, cost, and drug-interaction profile.

Supplements for bone

Vitamin D3, calcium, K2, magnesium — the bone-deposition triad.

Calcium and vitamin D do not prevent fractures or falls in community-dwelling adults who are not deficient. A 2026 BMJ meta-analysis of 69 trials and 153,902 adults (Massé et al., PMID 42161415, doi:10.1136/bmj-2025-088050) found a vitamin D fracture risk ratio of 1.00 (high certainty) and little to no effect on falls. Vitamin D still treats deficiency, and calcium still fills a dietary gap. Vitamin K2 (MK-7 100–200 mcg) has a separate bone-density trial, not a fracture trial. Discuss this with your provider if you have CKD or take warfarin.

Evidence summary

The numbers on the cards are the same composite as the supplement pages: efficacy, safety, research, onset, cost, and drug-interaction ratings, from 20 to 100. The strongest records on this page are Vitamin D3, Calcium, and Calcium carbonate/citrate (bone health). A higher score is a stronger record in the database, not a personal recommendation.

Who should be careful

  • Pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, and anyone taking a prescription drug should talk with a clinician before using this list.
  • Do not stack products from this list that sedate, thin the blood, or lower blood sugar with medicines that do the same thing.

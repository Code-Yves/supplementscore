Supplements for gut

Probiotics, prebiotics, and the fibre spectrum — what each strain actually does.

Probiotic effects are strain-specific, not generic. Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG (most-studied), Saccharomyces boulardii (only one that survives antibiotics), Bifidobacterium infantis 35624 (the IBS strain), and Lactobacillus reuteri DSM 17938 (infant colic) all have specific RCT-backed indications. Acacia fibre is the most gut-tolerant prebiotic. Psyllium has FDA health claims for both cholesterol and IBS-C. Peppermint oil (enteric-coated) is first-line for IBS in NICE guidelines. Avoid: 'multi-strain immunity blends' that don't specify strain or CFU.

Evidence summary

The numbers on the cards are the same composite as the supplement pages: efficacy, safety, research, onset, cost, and drug-interaction ratings, from 20 to 100. The strongest records on this page are Oat beta-glucan (cholesterol), Lactase enzyme, and Fibre (general dietary). A higher score is a stronger record in the database, not a personal recommendation.

Who should be careful

  • Pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, and anyone taking a prescription drug should talk with a clinician before using this list.
  • Do not stack products from this list that sedate, thin the blood, or lower blood sugar with medicines that do the same thing.

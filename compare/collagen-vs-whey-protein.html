Collagen vs whey protein

Last reviewed 21 October 2026 · SupplementScore editors · No sponsorships

Collagen is often sold as a protein powder. On the outcome that protein powders are bought for, muscle size during lifting, whey did better in the direct trial. Collagen is not useless as food. It is the weaker choice when the goal is muscle thickness.

WheyCollagen peptides
Muscle thickness, 10 weeks of liftingLarger gain in the leucine-matched comparisonSmaller gain
Strength and power in that trialImprovedImproved, no difference from whey
Leucine signal after exerciseHigher, in an 8-man acute studyLower
Skin wrinklesNot the product in the peptide reviewOral polypeptides drove a small pooled wrinkle change. Elasticity was inconsistent.
For muscle size, whey. For a skin-peptide question, collagen is in the category that has skin trials, and those trials are not a muscle result. Do not buy collagen expecting it to replace a protein powder.

The lifting trial

Jacinto 2022 (PMID 35042187, doi:10.1123/ijsnem.2021-0265) is the clean comparison. Twenty-two untrained young adults, 10 weeks, supervised lifting three days a week. Whey was 35 g with 3.0 g leucine. Collagen was 35 g with 1.0 g leucine plus 2.0 g free leucine, so the leucine dose was matched on purpose. Vastus lateralis thickness rose 8.4% with whey and 5.6% with collagen. Biceps thickness rose 10.1% and 6.0%. The interaction favored whey. Peak torque, mean power, and lower-body peak power rose over time with no group difference.

That sample is small and young and new to lifting. It is still a direct comparison, which most collagen advertisements are not.

A signaling study, not a 10-week body

Impey 2018 (PMID 29757056) gave 8 men 22 g of whey or a hydrolyzed collagen blend before, during, and after one cycling session done with low carbohydrate availability. Whey raised plasma leucine and post-exercise p70S6K1 activity more. Eight men and one workout cannot tell you which powder builds more muscle over a season. It does tell you collagen is a poor leucine source even when the scoop looks the same size.

A longer protein-versus-collagen comparison

Wageh 2024 (PMID 39475860) compared a multi-ingredient protein supplement with collagen. Satellite-cell responses to hard exercise and to longer resistance training were mostly similar. The protein supplement produced a greater increase in type II fiber cross-sectional area. "Multi-ingredient protein" is not plain whey, so this is supporting context, not a second copy of the Jacinto result.

Skin is a different question

Nukaly 2026 (PMID 41924746) meta-analyzed 19 randomized trials of oral and topical peptides, 1,341 people. Hydration and brightness improved. Wrinkles: mean difference 0.27 (p = 0.04), driven by oral polypeptides (mean difference 1.5, p = 0.01). Elasticity and density were inconsistent, and the products were well tolerated. This is not a finding that collagen builds muscle, and it is not only collagen: the review is peptides. Our existing collagen vs vitamin C page is the skin-specific comparison already on the site.

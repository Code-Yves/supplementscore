# SupplementScore — Content Opportunities

_Research date: 2026-06-06. Goal: find genuinely useful, non-duplicative additions — backed by real search demand and real evidence._

## TL;DR

The supplement catalog is already deep — **780 supplements** (33 strong-evidence, 303 promising, 347 trending, 97 risky), **66 conditions**, **127 comparisons**, **577 articles**. Spot-checks confirm even newer/trending compounds are present (lion's mane, beef-organ desiccated, urolithin A, taurine, fisetin, GLP-1-mimicking herbs). **So the opportunity is not more supplement entries** — it's _timely, use-case_ content where there's high public demand and the site currently has nothing.

The single biggest gap is the **GLP-1 / Ozempic companion** topic. After that: a handful of "what the newest research changed" articles, and a few high-demand lifestyle/timing use-cases.

## Tier 1 — High demand · strong evidence · clear gap

### 1. GLP-1 (Ozempic/Wegovy/Mounjaro) companion cluster  ⭐ flagship
**The gap:** the site covers supplements that *mimic* GLP-1, but nothing on what to take *while on* a GLP-1 drug — which is where the search volume actually is. ~12% of US adults have tried a GLP-1; "supplements with Ozempic / muscle loss on Ozempic" is one of the highest-volume health queries right now.

**Why it's on-brand & evidence-real:** PubMed shows active 2024-26 RCT/meta work on GLP-1 + muscle preservation + protein + resistance training; the deficiency and side-effect angles are well documented (Mayo Clinic, US News, GoodRx). This is exactly the "cut through the noise with evidence" job the site does.

Proposed pieces (a small hub + ~6 articles, all linking to existing supplement pages):
- **Hub / use-case page:** "Supplements on GLP-1 drugs: what the evidence supports" (new condition/use-case entry: `glp1_users`).
- Protein & muscle preservation on GLP-1 (protein, creatine, HMB, leucine) — muscle loss is the #1 concern.
- Micronutrient gaps on appetite suppression (vitamin D, B12, iron, A/E, calcium).
- Fiber & constipation on GLP-1 (psyllium, magnesium — you already have the satiety piece; this is the side-effect angle).
- Nausea & GI tolerance (ginger, electrolytes) — what helps vs. hype.
- Hair shedding during rapid weight loss (the truth on biotin/collagen/iron/zinc).
- "What NOT to stack with GLP-1 drugs" (safety: chromium, fiber timing, etc.).

### 2. "What the newest research changed" — breakthrough articles tied to 2025-26 studies
Your differentiator is *freshness* ("14,692 studies reviewed in the last 72 hours"). Lean into it with dated, single-study explainers. Each must cite the actual paper. Verified-real candidate topics (recent meta-analyses/RCTs exist on PubMed):
- Taurine & metabolic syndrome / blood pressure / glucose (2024-25 meta-analysis).
- Creatine & cognition — what the 2024-25 meta-analyses actually show (you have a brain-health piece; this is the "update" angle).
- The taurine-as-longevity-biomarker walk-back (raising a biomarker ≠ slowing aging — you already do this framing for NMN; extend it).
- Omega-3 + AFib risk vs. benefit at high dose (you have the data; a dated explainer pulls search traffic).

### 3. Build (or stop linking to) the condition pages you already reference
`data.js` lists `long_covid` and `cfs` as conditions and supplement pages *try* to link to `long-covid-evidence.html` / `me-cfs-evidence.html`, but those pages don't exist (I just removed the dead links). Both have **real, cautious evidence stories** (CoQ10/ubiquinol, D-ribose, L-carnitine) and meaningful search demand. Worth building properly — then re-add the cross-links.

- Duplicating single-supplement explainers you already have.

How SupplementScore is made

SupplementScore is openly mostly AI-generated. The pages are drawn from the same credible sources doctors rely on: PubMed, the Cochrane Library, ClinicalTrials.gov, the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, the FDA, EFSA, EMA, and Health Canada.

Who writes the pages

Language models draft the supplement cards, the articles, the comparisons, and the condition pages. A person sets the scoring rules, decides what is published, and corrects mistakes readers flag. The same public sources named above are the raw material: peer-reviewed papers and regulator documents, not ads and not affiliate reviews.

The site is bringing physicians on board. No physician signs a page before it is published, and a page does not carry a medical-review label. This is an educational reference. It is not personal medical advice. Scoring rules live on the methodology page.

Study count: 3,032

One study is one PubMed ID. The same ID on many pages counts once.

The count reads the site’s pages and data files and keeps each PubMed ID once. That scan found 3,032 distinct IDs. The procedure is scripts/transparency_counts.py in the public repository.

Full file rules

Read every .html, .js, and .json file in the site repository, except node_modules, tests, scripts, outputs, _archive, reviews, _mockups, og, dist, _docs, fr, es, and any filename containing “mockup”. Keep an ID when the text has PMID followed by a 6- to 9-digit number, or a pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ link with that number.

Estimated research hours: about 11,000

This is an estimate of the time one person would need to find, read, and appraise the cited studies, and to research and write the published pages. It is not time people spent inside the trials, and it is not a timesheet of hours already worked.

Formula. 3,032 distinct studies × 1.5 hours, plus 1,633 published pages × 4 hours.

3,032 × 1.5 = 4,548 hours for the studies. 1,633 × 4 = 6,532 hours for the pages. Together that is 11,080 hours, which rounds to the nearest thousand: about 11,000 hours.

Assumptions.

  • 1.5 hours per study to find it, read it, and appraise it. A 2021 scoping review in the Journal of Clinical Epidemiology (Nussbaumer-Streit and colleagues, doi:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2021.05.019) reported that pulling the main facts from one study took one person about 41 to 65 minutes, and a short critical-appraisal checklist took about 10 to 21 minutes more. 1.5 hours (90 minutes) is a round figure near that band, with a little time left to find the paper. That review did not measure 1.5 hours, and it did not time this site.
  • 4 hours per published page to research and write the page after the studies are already counted, so those study hours are not added a second time inside the page hours. The same 4 hours is used for every page type below, from a short brand page to a long stack. No published timing study was found for writing a supplement page. 4 hours is a stated assumption.

Pages counted: 1,633. These are published English pages. Directory indexes and short redirect stubs are left out.

  • 734 supplement pages
  • 609 articles

A page counts when it is an HTML file in one of those folders, it is not named index.html, and the top of the file is not a refresh redirect. The same script produces the study count and these page counts.

This is an estimate. A faster reader, or a page that mostly repeats studies already counted, would land lower. A page that needed extra safety checking would land higher.

Money and corrections

There are no ads, no sponsors, and no affiliate links. Who pays for hosting is in About — Funding. To flag a wrong dose, study, or safety line, use Flag an error or write to editorial@supplementscore.org with the page, the sentence, and a public source.

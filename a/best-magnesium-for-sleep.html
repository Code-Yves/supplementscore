Best magnesium for sleep

By the SupplementScore team · Updated 15 October 2026 · No ads or affiliate links
Bottom line

No head-to-head trial shows that magnesium glycinate beats magnesium citrate for sleep. The insomnia trial people quote most often used magnesium in older adults and was not a glycinate study. Glycinate is a reasonable pick when oxide or citrate upsets the stomach. Citrate is the form with clearer absorption data and a laxative effect. Read the elemental magnesium line, and stay near the 350 mg a day supplemental upper limit unless a clinician sets a different dose.

Sleep labels lean on the word glycinate. The capsule weight is usually the whole salt, not the magnesium. A 400 mg glycinate capsule often holds far less elemental magnesium than 400 mg. That is the number that matters for magnesium glycinate and for magnesium citrate.

What the sleep trials actually used

A double-blind trial in older adults with primary insomnia found better sleep measures with magnesium than with placebo (Abbasi and colleagues, 2012, PMID 23853635). It did not establish glycinate as the winning form. A 2021 systematic review of oral magnesium for insomnia in older adults (PMID 33865376) and a 2023 review of magnesium and sleep (PMID 35184264) both describe a modest signal, mostly in people who sleep poorly, not a guarantee for every healthy adult. A 2024 review of supplemental magnesium, anxiety, and sleep (PMID 38817505) is in the same direction and is still not a form-versus-form test.

Glycinate, citrate, and oxide

Citrate has come out ahead of other common salts on absorption in a randomized comparison (PMID 14596323). Commercial oxide preparations are the poorly absorbed end of the range (PMID 11794633). An updated review of magnesium absorption makes the same practical point: the salt and the dose both change how much you take up (PMID 29123461). Glycinate is popular because it is easier on the gut than oxide and less likely than citrate to loosen stools. That is a tolerability reason, not a sleep-trial win.

Glycine is a separate question

Glycine itself, at a multi-gram dose, has a small sleep study (PMID 22293292). The glycine riding along in a typical glycinate capsule is a fraction of that dose. Do not treat a magnesium capsule as if it were a glycine sleep trial. The glycine card is the place for that ingredient.

A sensible way to choose

If sleep is the goal and your stomach is fine, citrate is the form with the better absorption record. If citrate sends you to the bathroom, glycinate is the usual switch. Oxide is the one to stop buying for this purpose. Take it in the evening if that is when you want the effect, and do not stack several magnesium products until the elemental total is clear. People with kidney disease need advice before any extra magnesium.

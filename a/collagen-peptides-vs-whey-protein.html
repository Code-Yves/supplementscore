Collagen peptides vs whey protein

By the SupplementScore team · Updated 15 October 2026 · No ads or affiliate links
Bottom line

Use whey when the job is daily protein and muscle. Collagen peptides are low in essential amino acids, especially tryptophan, so they do not replace whey or food protein. One trial in older adults with sarcopenia found that collagen plus resistance training changed body composition. That is not a finding that collagen beats whey. If you want both, count collagen as extra, not as the protein target.

Whey protein is a complete protein. Collagen peptides are a partial one. Comparing them as if they were two flavors of the same tub is how people under-eat essential amino acids.

Muscle protein

The ISSN protein stand (PMID 28642676) and the Morton meta-analysis of protein plus lifting (PMID 28698222) are about reaching a daily protein intake. Whey is a convenient way to do that because it contains the essential amino acids muscle protein synthesis uses. Collagen does not match that amino acid pattern.

What the collagen trial showed

Zdzieblik and colleagues gave specific collagen peptides plus resistance training to older men with sarcopenia and reported better body composition and strength than training alone (PMID 26353786). The control was not whey. The people in the trial were not young athletes swapping shakes. Skin and joint claims live on the collagen card and should be read there, at the dose the trial used, not at "a scoop instead of dinner."

If you already take whey

Adding collagen on top does not require you to drop whey. Replacing whey with collagen does. Creatine vs whey is the other common mix-up, and creatine is a third ingredient, not a protein.

Sources

  1. Jäger R, et al. International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: protein and exercise. J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2017. PMID: 28642676.
  2. Morton RW, et al. Protein supplementation and resistance training-induced gains. Br J Sports Med. 2018. PMID: 28698222.
  3. Zdzieblik D, et al. Collagen peptide supplementation in combination with resistance training improves body composition and increases muscle strength in elderly sarcopenic men. Br J Nutr. 2015. PMID: 26353786.

How Much Vitamin D Do Adults Need?

6 min read · By the SupplementScore team · Updated 15 October 2026 · No ads or affiliate links
Bottom Line

The adult allowance is 600 IU of vitamin D a day from ages 1 to 70, and 800 IU from age 71. The adult upper limit is 4,000 IU a day. In the 2011–2014 US survey, about 5% of people were below 12 ng/mL and about 18% were between 12 and 20 ng/mL. That is not a finding that 40% of adults are deficient, and it is not a reason to take 5,000 IU every day. You do not need a vitamin K2 pill just because you take vitamin D. Anyone on warfarin should not add vitamin K on their own.

What blood level counts as low

Vitamin D status is the blood level of 25-hydroxyvitamin D, written 25(OH)D. The National Academies, as restated by the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, treat 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L) or higher as enough for most people. Risk of deficiency rises below 12 ng/mL (30 nmol/L). Levels from 12 to 20 ng/mL are a possible shortfall, not automatic proof of disease. Levels above 50 ng/mL (125 nmol/L) can be linked to harm.

An analysis of the 2011–2014 National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey found that 5% of people age 1 year and older were below 12 ng/mL and 18% were in the 12 to 20 ng/mL range. Older surveys that put the share below 20 ng/mL near 40% are not the current national picture. The 2011 Endocrine Society guideline used different cutoffs, including a goal above 30 ng/mL. The NIH fact sheet now says the Endocrine Society has not set sufficiency cutoffs and does not recommend routine 25(OH)D testing.

How much vitamin D per day

The recommended dietary allowance is 15 micrograms (600 IU) a day for ages 1 to 70 and 20 micrograms (800 IU) a day from age 71. Those figures assume little sun. The adult tolerable upper intake level is 100 micrograms (4,000 IU) a day. A 1,000 IU or 2,000 IU vitamin D3 product sits under that upper limit. A 5,000 IU product does not, unless a clinician is treating a measured deficiency.

The Endocrine Society recommends routine vitamin D for children and teens ages 1 to 18, during pregnancy, for adults with prediabetes, and for adults 75 and older. It does not recommend routine supplementation for healthy adults ages 19 to 74, and it does not set its own dose. It says people should stay with the allowance above. Winter sun at northern latitudes is a weak source. The allowance and the Canadian context are in vitamin D in a Canadian winter.

On 15 October 2026, the public USP Verified list included Nature Made vitamin D3 at 1,000 IU and 2,000 IU, and Member's Mark vitamin D3 at 2,000 IU. Member's Mark also had a 5,000 IU softgel on that list. A seal says that bottle matched the directory. It does not make a dose above 4,000 IU a default. Vitamin D2 or vitamin D3

A 2012 meta-analysis of trials that compared the two forms found that vitamin D3 raised 25(OH)D more than vitamin D2 overall. That difference was clear for large single doses and was not significant when both forms were taken every day. Daily D3 is the form in most of the certified products above. It is not a rule that every daily D2 product fails.

Do you need vitamin K2 as well?

No. Taking vitamin D does not create a requirement for vitamin K2. The randomized AVADEC trial of vitamin K2 plus vitamin D did not slow aortic valve calcification. The plain-language version is do you need vitamin K2 with vitamin D. Vitamin K changes how warfarin works. A K2 softgel is not a casual add-on for anyone on that drug. See supplements to avoid with warfarin and the interaction checker.

When a blood test helps

Severe deficiency can cause rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults. The NIH fact sheet does not treat a level of 20 to 30 ng/mL as a cause of infections, mood disorders, or weak immunity. The US Preventive Services Task Force has said the evidence is not enough to judge routine vitamin D screening in adults without symptoms, and no national professional group recommends screening everyone.

Low vitamin D is more common with little sun, with darker skin, with diets that skip fortified foods, and when the gut does not absorb fat well, including some liver disease, cystic fibrosis, celiac disease, and inflammatory bowel disease. A test is how a clinician finds a deficiency. This page is not a target number and not a prescription. Vitamin D toxicity, with high calcium and, in extreme cases, kidney injury, comes from too much supplement, not from ordinary food.

Mild-to-Moderate Depression: The Evidence-Based Supplement Protocol

7 min read ·
Bottom Line

Saffron, EPA-dominant omega-3, SAMe, and St. John's wort (with interaction warnings) — the supplements with randomized-trial evidence for mild-to-moderate depression.

For moderate-to-severe depression, evidence-based psychotherapy and antidepressants are first-line, and no supplement replaces them. But for mild-to-moderate symptoms — or as a clinician-supervised add-on — a few supplements have real randomized-trial support: saffron, EPA-dominant omega-3, SAMe, St. John's wort, and vitamin D when it is low. The single most important caveat — St. John's wort's drug interactions — comes up front. Here is what the trials show.

Saffron, 30 mg Daily

Saffron (Crocus sativus) has the cleanest profile of the herbal options. A meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials found saffron produced a large reduction in depressive symptoms versus placebo and performed about as well as standard antidepressants in head-to-head trials, at a typical dose of 30 mg/day of a standardized extract. Most trials are short (6–8 weeks), small, and concentrated in Iran, so independent replication and longer follow-up are still needed — but for mild-to-moderate depression it is a reasonable, well-tolerated option. Side effects are usually mild (GI upset, mild sedation). See our saffron vs St. John's wort comparison.

Omega-3, EPA-Dominant ~1 g EPA Daily

The omega-3 evidence is genuinely mixed, and the details matter. An early meta-analysis found that most of the apparent benefit disappeared after correcting for publication bias. But subsequent analyses clarified that formulation is decisive: preparations that are pure EPA or at least 60% EPA, at doses up to about 1 g/day of EPA, show a clinical benefit, whereas DHA-dominant products do not. So if you try omega-3 for mood, use an EPA-dominant EPA/DHA product rather than a generic fish oil, set modest expectations, and treat it as an adjunct — the effect is real but small and inconsistent.

SAMe, 800–1,600 mg Daily

SAMe (S-adenosylmethionine) is involved in the methylation reactions that make neurotransmitters. A clinician-oriented systematic review found SAMe outperformed placebo in several trials and matched the antidepressants imipramine and escitalopram in others, both as monotherapy and as an add-on to SSRIs, at daily doses of roughly 800–1,600 mg. It is generally well tolerated but can cause insomnia or activation, and — critically — it can trigger mania in people with bipolar disorder and may add to serotonergic load when combined with antidepressants. Use it under supervision if you are already on an antidepressant.

St. John's Wort, 300 mg (0.3% Hypericin) Three Times Daily — Interaction Warning First

St. John's wort has the largest evidence base of any supplement here: a systematic review of 35 trials in over 6,900 patients found it superior to placebo (relative risk of response ~1.5) and not significantly different from antidepressants for mild-to-moderate depression, with fewer side effects than prescription drugs. The reason it is not a casual recommendation is its interactions. St. John's wort is a potent inducer of CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein, so it lowers blood levels of many critical drugs — oral contraceptives (risk of unintended pregnancy), warfarin, immunosuppressants, some HIV and cancer drugs, and more — and combined with SSRIs it can cause serotonin syndrome. Never start it without checking every medication you take with a pharmacist.

Vitamin D, 1,000–2,000 IU Daily (Only If Low)

Low vitamin D is associated with depression, but supplementation mainly helps people who are genuinely deficient; in people with normal levels, trials show little mood benefit. If your 25-hydroxyvitamin D is low, correcting it to a normal range is worthwhile for several reasons and may modestly help mood; if it is already normal, more is not better. It is a deficiency-correction step, not a stand-alone antidepressant.

What NOT to Take

Do not combine St. John's wort, SAMe, saffron, or 5-HTP with an SSRI/SNRI without medical supervision — stacking serotonergic agents risks serotonin syndrome. Do not use any of these as the sole treatment for severe depression, psychotic features, or active suicidal thoughts — that needs urgent professional care. Avoid St. John's wort entirely if you take oral contraceptives, anticoagulants, transplant drugs, or antiretrovirals. And do not stop a prescribed antidepressant to "go natural" without a tapering plan from your clinician.

How to Run the Protocol

If symptoms are mild-to-moderate and you are not on interacting drugs, saffron 30 mg/day is the simplest, best-tolerated starting point; St. John's wort 300 mg of a 0.3%-hypericin extract three times daily is comparably effective if — and only if — a pharmacist clears your medication list. Add EPA-dominant omega-3 (~1 g EPA/day) as an adjunct, and correct vitamin D only if it is measured low. Give any single agent 6–8 weeks and track symptoms with a simple scale. If there is no meaningful improvement, or if symptoms are worsening, escalate to professional treatment rather than stacking more supplements. Keep psychotherapy and clinician follow-up in the picture throughout.
